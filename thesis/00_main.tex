% Numerical claims are common in political discourse, news, and social media, yet their verification remains difficult even within a single language. In multilingual settings, this challenge is amplified: large language models often fail to treat semantically equivalent claims consistently, so a claim and its translation may receive different reasoning-trace rankings and, consequently, different verdicts. This thesis investigates whether optimizing explicitly for language-invariant reasoning yields genuine improvements in reasoning quality and ranking performance, or merely produces more stable surface behavior. We study this question using CLEF CheckThat! 2026 Task 2, which requires ranking candidate reasoning traces for claims in English, Spanish, and Arabic and deriving a final verdict from the highest-ranked traces. Building on our shared-task submission, a Llama-3.1-8B trace scorer fine-tuned with Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA) that ranked first in English and third in Spanish, we compare prompting-based ranking, Direct Preference Optimization with English-only and multilingual preference data, and supervised trace scoring. Across these approaches, we evaluate both task performance, using Recall@5, MRR@5, and macro-F1, and cross-lingual consistency, using agreement between model judgments over parallel claims.
